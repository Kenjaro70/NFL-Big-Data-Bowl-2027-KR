## Method

- **Routes:** every WR route in a regular-season play (penalty-nullified plays dropped). The window runs from the snap to 1 s after the throw, both taken from the receiver's own tracking event tags.
- **Cuts:** the Phase 1 detector runs unchanged, so game and combine breaks get identical smoothing, thresholds and metrics. A cut counts as a route break if its apex comes no later than 0.5 s after the throw (on timing routes the QB releases before the break), at least 1 yd past the receiver's snap position (shallower turns are release moves at the line), and at least 1.5 s after the snap, so the whole 1.5 s entry window is route running rather than the stance or pre-snap motion. Screens are left out: they have no route break.
- **Break side:** `in` turns toward the middle of the field, `out` toward the sideline. The ball isn't tracked, so the middle of the field stands in for it.
- **Scores:** slot = route × break side. Game breaks in a slot still differ in depth and angle (a 5-yd quick out vs a 12-yd deep out), which drives speed and retention. So within each slot every metric is regressed on depth and turn angle (with squares), and the standardized residual is the break's score. A player's game feature is the mean score over their breaks, the same construction as the combine features.
- **Outcomes:** separation at the throw minus the cohort average for the same route, coverage (man / zone) and time to throw = separation over expected (SOE). Usage and production: target rate, yards per route run.
- **Reliability:** split-half across games (each player's games split at random, 200 times, Spearman–Brown corrected), for qualified WRs (≥ 100 regular-season routes). A few low-volume WRs have such noisy averages that including them drags every reliability down.
- **Rookie season:** every game feature and outcome also exists for the rookie season alone, which every draft class has exactly once.

## Which detected cuts count as route breaks

Reasons overlap, so the middle rows don't add up. Cuts in the first 1.5 s are mostly release moves and receivers in motion turning upfield: 41% are less than 1 yd deep, and 31% come from a receiver in motion at the snap (vs 6% of later cuts). Their entry speed and braking are measured partly on the stance or the motion, so how often a WR has one reflects their role, not how they cut. ## Detectability of Phase 3's player-level tests

Measurement noise caps any observed correlation at √(reliability × reliability). With the WRs available, a two-sided test at α = 0.05 with 80% power needs the observed |r| in the 6th column, so the true correlation has to be at least the last column. This uses reliabilities only: no combine feature has been correlated with game data or outcomes. Route-level models of SOE (tens of thousands of routes, player random effects) are not limited the same way.
